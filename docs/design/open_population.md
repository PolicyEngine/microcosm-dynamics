## In brief

This lane owns four rows of the parity ledger (`docs/parity-ledger.md`): births, immigration and emigration, alignment and a representative population, and projections beyond 2030. The only price on record for any of it is design B's B8 (2032/2050 horizons plus population extension, income and wealth gates included): 40–70 d, an estimate made before the limits in §2.4 were written down.

The design splits the work in two:

- **A demographic phase that can start now.** Newborns with both parent links, immigrant cohorts sized to the Trustees' gross inflow, an emigration law, an alignment layer that keeps the unaligned path as the scored object, a 2013→2020 retrodiction of the population's composition, and the demographic projection to 2100. None of it needs forward earnings, so none of it waits on Track B. Ten items (OP0.1, OP1 to OP7, OP10a, OP11a), **76–113 d** of effort (estimate), about 46–69 engineer-day equivalents elapsed on two lanes.
- **An economic phase that waits on Track B.** Entrant initialization (OP8: entrants' starting states, earnings state, covered-work histories and insured status, and newborns' earnings at 16–24 with the hand-off to Track B's earnings law at 25); alignment to labor targets; the economic long horizon; matched 2050, 2065 and 2070 comparisons; and a gate on SSA benefit and payment totals. Six items (OP0.2, OP8, OP9, OP10b, OP11b, OP12), **64–103 d** (estimate). They depend on Track B's H, B3, B4 and J, so none but OP0.2 can start until a successor to B2 passes (ruling d1659). The modeled path to OP11b ends about 68–109 engineer-day equivalents after that day, if Track B's B0.2 (draft PR #554) has frozen by then and Track B keeps its own estimates. OP12 has no finite date while the Microcosm start's gate 3, which it needs, waits on parked decision d947 (§3.4).

**All 15 milestone rows (OP0 counts once): 140–216 d, or 35–54 lw (estimate).** **Entrant initialization, which Track B §3.3 left unpriced, is its own row, OP8: 26–41 d, or 6.5–10.25 lw (estimate).** It builds on demographic packets that OP2 and OP3 already pay for (9–13 d), so its full price is 35–54 d (§3.5). Insured status itself comes from Track B's insured-status rule, which OP8 consumes through the interface in §6.1 and does not price.

Three findings shape the design:

1. The certified M6 assembly builds its marital, disability and household panels from native PSID sources over a predeclared anchor universe, ignoring the live projection frame, and simulates each once per draw; each year it redraws a whole-window fertility history over the cached marital panel; and its native panel support is pinned through 2022 and supplies no admitted forward support afterward (§2.4). A synthetic entrant absent from that support, a newborn or an immigrant, cannot marry, give birth, become disabled or join a household through it. Open-population processes therefore run through forward-mode adapters, which Track B's B4 owns for incumbents.
2. Scheduled entrants make a projection's early years depend on its end year: on invented data, runs to 2035 and to 2045 differ from 2026 on, while the same runs without entrants are identical (§2.9). Production runs therefore use one registered horizon and read shorter horizons as prefixes.
3. Two earlier designs, for immigration (#218) and alignment (#217), closed unmerged on 25 September 2026. This design keeps their core rulings, prices them and adds what they left out: newborns' links and states, an emigration law, a population-composition gate and the horizon (§2.8).

### Terms

- **ACS, CPS ASEC, PUMS.** The Census Bureau's American Community Survey; the Current Population Survey's Annual Social and Economic Supplement; and the public-use microdata files of each.
- **ASFR, TFR.** Age-specific fertility rate (births per woman at an age) and total fertility rate (the sum of a year's ASFRs).
- **AWI.** SSA's national average wage index.
- **EPUF.** SSA's 2006 Earnings Public-Use File: covered earnings for 1951–2006 for a 1 percent sample of Social Security numbers.
- **OACT, V.A1 to V.A4, IV.B4, V.B1.** SSA's Office of the Chief Actuary and tables of the 2026 Trustees Report.
- **Quarters of coverage.** SSA's units of covered work, up to four a year, from which insured status is decided.
- **`u_w`.** The forward earnings law's estimate of a person's long-run earnings rank, fitted from the normalized ranks of their positive earnings (`engine/forward_earnings.py:697–712`) and blended into the law's draws (`:1528–1533`).
- **Gate, report-only, floor.** A gate is a pass-or-fail test registered before it runs; a report-only check is published the same way with no pass or fail; a floor is the spread between two disjoint samples of real data, from which a tolerance is priced.

## 1. Purpose

Each ledger row states the test that would show parity. This design plans the milestones that would meet each test, with a gate or an explicit report-only label at every step.

| Ledger row | The row's test (from `docs/parity-ledger.md`) |
|---|---|
| Births | Projected births against vital statistics; newborns' links to both parents, households and starting states; their entry into later earnings, disability and benefit processes |
| Immigration and emigration | Arrivals and departures sized to OACT's assumptions; entrants' covered-work histories and insured status; their benefit receipt against SSA statistics |
| Alignment and a representative population | An alignment step to the actuaries' demographic and labor targets that keeps households consistent, with its displacement from the unaligned path published; gated SSA benefit and payment totals |
| Projections beyond 2030 | Near-term gates first (gate 3); then matched 2050, 2065 and 2070 comparisons and 75-year sensitivity surfaces on named assumption paths, each registered before it runs |

Track B's rules carry over unchanged (Track B §1, §5.1): each mechanism, population and horizon is admitted separately, through a registered gate against independent observations; no DYNASIM comparison may select structure, parameters, denominators or rows; DYNASIM agreement never rescues a failed gate.

One rule specific to this lane comes from M6's decision 9, which the closed alignment design quotes ("Alignment layer report-only; gate the un-aligned drift", PR #217 §1.2): **the unaligned projection is the scientific object that every gate scores; the aligned projection is a labeled production companion whose displacement is published.** This design applies the rule to every gate, OP12 included. Gating an aligned scenario would need an explicit exception from Max (§10, decision 10).

### 2.1 The loop and its seams

- **Eight adapters in a fixed order.** `engine/loop.py:117–129` defines `PeriodModules`: mortality, aging, marital core, fertility, disability, earnings, claiming and household composition, plus an optional initializer. The loop calls them in that order each year (`loop.py:297–350`).
- **No emigration or alignment step.** None of the eight adapters removes a living person or reconciles to an external target.
- **One entry seam.** Scheduled entrants arrive through `metadata["m6_scheduled_entries_by_year"]` (`loop.py:27`). The loop validates each frame (`:226–240`), rejects IDs that appear twice (`:242–250`), and appends the year's entrants before that year's mortality draw (`:271–286`).
- **Identifiers and random streams.** A projection-wide `SyntheticPersonIdAllocator` hands out new IDs and refuses reserved real ones (`loop.py:43–64`; created at `:251–262` unless the caller supplies one). Each person's stream is keyed by an ordinal: initial and scheduled IDs get ordinals in sorted order before the first year (`:263–266`); anyone else, such as a newborn, gets the next ordinal at the end of the year they appear (`:358–360`). The stream for a period and module does not depend on the projection's length: `rng.py:61–67` takes the `period`-th child of a fresh root sequence.
- **Sealed files.** `engine/loop.py` and `engine/steps.py` sit inside the source identity that the first-estimates birth-evidence reducer seals: every file under the package outside `POST_REVIEW_SOURCE_EXCLUSIONS` must equal reviewed commit `1c7f3540` (`ss/statutory_aime.py:137–141`; the exclusion tuple starts at `scripts/first_estimates_birth_evidence.py:135`, and `entrant_schedule.py` and `entrant_domains.py` are on it at `:176–178`). Track B §5.1 also forbids edits to `engine/loop.py`, `engine/steps.py`, `gates.yaml` and committed `runs/*.json`. Every new module this lane adds must join the exclusion tuple, which `tests/estimates/test_birth_evidence_artifact.py:68` pins exactly, and pass its transitive reachability test (`:367`) and the initializer-coverage test (`:336`).

### 2.2 Births

- **The closed projections draw none.** The registered Track A and FRA-68 projections are closed cohorts whose fertility slot draws no births (`baselines/fertility.py:8–11`).
- **Gate m6's engine materializes them, report-only.** Its reference draw added 316 newborns (`runs/gate_m6_candidate3_v1.json`, `family_b.entrants.family_b_open_additions.synthetic_births`), and the gate names entrants, births and open-panel margins as report-only (`gates.yaml:5416–5420`).
- **What a newborn gets.** `steps.materialize_maternal_births` (`steps.py:451–507`) gives each child a new ID from the projection's allocator, age 0, its birth year, a sex drawn 50/50 from the fertility module's stream (`:494–496`), a link to the mother (`:497`), `synthetic_entry = True` (`:498`), and the mother's `household_id`, `weight` and `start_weight` (`:499–503`). Every other roster column is missing, because the child rows are built on the roster's columns and only these are filled (`:485–487`). There is no second-parent link; the paternal "shadow" births are household conditioning draws and are deliberately not materialized (`:459–461`).
- **Timing.** Births are appended in step 4, after step 1's deaths, so no newborn can die in its birth year; its first mortality draw comes the next year at age 0 (`loop.py:297–323`; `steps.py:113–134`).
- **The gated fertility kernel.** Candidate 16's maternal-birth kernel draws each woman-year from her age, parity and birth decade (`engine/marital.py:376–382`), with parity starting at zero when the simulation starts (`:363`). In the M6 assembly, each projected period redraws a whole-window fertility history over the cached marital panel using that period's RNG, resets parity to zero for that invocation, and materializes only the current year's maternal births (`engine/assembly.py:301–329`; `steps.py:538–561`; `marital.py:363–391`).
- **The opt-in ASFR step.** `baselines/fertility.py` draws one Bernoulli birth per exposed woman-year at a baseline's single-age rate, at most one birth a year (`:14–22`), from a person-keyed tagged stream (`:24–28`), and materializes children through the same function (`:181`). It is labeled "not gated, report-only" (`:59`), and no registered test uses it (`:8–12`).

### 2.3 Scheduled entrants

- **A report-only cohort builder.** `engine/entrant_schedule.py` turns a donor frame and annual controls in thousands into seam-ready frames (`:185–396`). It sizes cohorts to the Trustees' gross positive inflow (LPR inflow plus temporary-or-unlawfully-present inflow) and excludes outflow, adjustment of status and total net change (`:37–42`, `:359–364`). It reweights donors rather than resampling them, so it consumes no random draws (`:44–48`). Its provenance says `gated: False`, `report_only: True` (`:387–388`).
- **Its timing convention is the seam's.** An entrant for year Y carries `year = Y − 1` and `age = entry_age`, faces a mortality draw at the entry age, then ages (`:14–22`; `docs/entrant-seam.md`, "Existing loop timing").
- **That timing double-filters one control.** The temporary-or-unlawfully-present inflow counts only people who remain to year-end (`entrant_schedule.py:365–371`), so a full-year mortality draw at the seam, and any emigration in the entry year, would filter that component's survival a second time. PR #218 blocks production until a component-aware timing and exposure bridge exists (its design at head `152d087e`, lines 389–414 and 1235–1246). OP3 prices that bridge.
- **What it lacks.** The readers for controls and donors were not carried over (`entrant_schedule.py:41–42`; `docs/entrant-seam.md:1–7`), so no committed run has added an immigrant; gate m6 records `immigrant_cohorts: 0`, a value its runner writes as a constant (`harness/m6_runner.py:1027`, `:1212`).
- **Entrant support restrictions are declared.** `engine/entrant_domains.py:49–74` lists four exclusions and why: fertility risk (no parity seed), claiming (no insured-status test), the disability panel (entrants are not PSID panel members) and the earnings domain (no fitted 2014 state). Benefit outputs for entrants are blanked to missing, never zero (`:210–228`); a claiming wrapper runs the claiming step on incumbents only (`:312–380`); and the exclusion report says `execution_verified: False` (`:244–262`). These inventories do not demonstrate that every restriction executes in a projection (`:1–12`; `docs/entrant-seam.md:59–65`); only the claiming wrapper enforces its own contract.
- **ID reuse is checked outside the loop.** `validate_projection_allocator` refuses metadata whose allocator could reuse a known ID (`entrant_schedule.py:457–507`). The loop does not check it, because changing `loop.py` needs a re-pin of the reducer seal (`:472–476`).

### 2.4 What the M6 assembly can and cannot carry for people who enter later

`engine/assembly.py:234–447` wires the certified fitted objects into the eight slots:

- **Marital core:** the panel builder ignores the live frame and builds candidate 16's seed panel from native sources over the full anchor universe (`engine/panel_builders.py:150–167`; the household builder likewise, `:257–274`); the assembly simulates it once per draw for the whole window and caches it (`assembly.py:282–299`). Realized PSID openers who enter in 2017 and 2019 are predeclared in that support (`harness/m6_population.py:339–350`).
- **Fertility:** each period, a whole-window redraw over the cached marital panel for the cached marital IDs, materializing that period's births (§2.2; `assembly.py:301–329`).
- **Disability:** a reproduction over realized PSID support rows for the window, simulated once (`:340–379`).
- **Household composition:** built and simulated once (`:403–422`).
- **Earnings:** the forward law behind the earnings-domain wrapper (`:243`, `:390–391`); people without the fitted 2014 state get the pinned non-scored zero (`engine/earnings_domain.py:1–7`).
- **Claiming:** `apply_claiming`, which draws a claim age for everyone 50 or older with no insured-status test (`steps.py:390–448`, `:411–413`).

The cached updates join the live roster by a left merge on `person_id` (`_merge_period_columns`, `assembly.py:174–192`), so a person absent from a nonempty update gets missing marital, disability and household values. The panel builders accept only inputs whose declared projection end year is 2022 (`engine/panel_builders.py:27`, `:128–131`), so the native panel support is pinned through 2022 and supplies no admitted forward support afterward; for a year with no cached update, the merge returns the frame unchanged (`assembly.py:181–183`).

**Consequence.** A synthetic entrant absent from the predeclared support, a newborn or an immigrant, cannot marry, give birth, become disabled or join a household through the certified assembly, and its native support ends in 2022. Open-population processes need forward-mode, live-roster adapters. Track B's B4 owns that successor for incumbents: forward support, redrawn initial states, and M6's marriage and divorce composed with death-triggered widowhood (Track B §3.1). B4 also declares closed-population versus entrant coverage (Track B §3.4). The forward support contract already exists and refuses realized holdout inputs (`engine/support.py:236–252`).

### 2.9 A finding: with scheduled entrants, early years depend on the end year

`docs/design/open_population_evidence/horizon_dependence.py` runs the existing loop on an invented 400-person roster to 2035 and to 2045, once with births only and once with immigrant cohorts scheduled for every year through the end year. Its committed output (`horizon_dependence.txt`) shows the closed runs' slices identical in every shared year, 2026–2035, and the open runs' slices different in every shared year, with row counts diverging from 2027.

The mechanism follows from §2.1: every scheduled entrant through the end year gets an ordinal before the first year, so a later end year moves every newborn's ordinal, and with it the newborn's random stream; the allocator's starting ID moves too. Without entrants, ordinals do not depend on the end year.

**Consequence for this design.** A production projection uses one registered horizon (the baseline's last year, 2100 for TR2026) and reads shorter horizons as prefixes of that run. OP1 enforces this, and §7 makes it a property test. Gate runs have their own fixed horizons and are never compared across horizons. Stable ID namespaces with adapter-owned ordinals are a possible later alternative; nothing shows it is cheaper.

### 3.6 Still unpriced

These must be priced before a complete budget is approved:
- an entrant forward disability model and its gate (Track B's B4 limits DI outcomes; nothing priced here gives entrants a forward disability hazard);
- a test of the age-25 transition for entrants' generated histories (their joint earnings histories and the hand-off into Track B's law), unless J's frozen estimands cover these entrants;
- an entrant-specific test of benefit receipt (for example against the ACS's Social Security income by nativity and year of entry);
- a bridge for §214(c)'s criterion for noncitizens: when the person's first Social Security number was assigned (before 1 January 2004 exempts them), and otherwise whether a number for work purposes was issued at any time since or the person was ever admitted as a business visitor or crewman (POMS RS 00301.102). No public survey records these, so until a bridge exists the insured-status rule may refuse many noncitizen entrants' statuses by name (§6.1);
- citizenship changes after entry (naturalization), which this design holds fixed at the entry state;
- totalization agreements, under which some immigrants count foreign coverage toward insured status;
- a covered-earnings bridge for temporary or unlawfully present entrants, whose covered share the ACS does not observe;
- an "abroad" ledger that keeps insured emigrants' benefits in national totals (OP4 records exits and OP12 counts insured emigrants; paying them is a separate deliverable);
- moving the engine into Microcosm as an operator (#412), which the Microcosm start also leaves out;
- data-access time for any source OP0 finds restricted (OACT single-age fertility or population, if they must be requested);
- review and registration queues.

## 5. Admission criteria and registration discipline

### 5.1 General rules

These apply to every OP milestone, in addition to Track B §5.1:
- Each milestone adds new modules and a new registered version; it edits no sealed file, no `track_b/` file, no `gates.yaml` cell and no committed run.
- Every gate, OP12 included, runs through an unaligned-only facade whose request cannot carry a target bundle; the aligned producer is outside the gate runner's import closure (#217 §4.1). Only Max can make an exception (§10, decision 10).
- Sources, module versions, seeds, populations, estimands, tolerances and alternatives are frozen before any real-data outcome is computed. Output paths are exclusive, and abort records are published.
- Every production run uses one registered horizon (§2.9).
- Every milestone reports its verification class and admitted scope (§3.2).

### 5.2 What OP0 freezes

OP0 produces one frozen record per gate, with Track B §5.2's elements: source release, variables, population, exposure history, holdout partition, critical cells, numerical tolerances, uncertainty treatment, pass conjunction and the named power procedure. It adds four elements this lane needs:
- **the universe** of each source and its bridge to the projection's roster (Social Security area, resident population, civilian noninstitutional population, group quarters);
- **the date mapping** from each source's reference date or collection period (a calendar-year survey, a 1 April census, a July 1 estimate) to the projection's annual slices;
- **the target manifest**: for each external target, its table, row, column, vintage, captured-byte hash and status (`available`, `not_available` or `validation_only`), as #217 §7.1 specifies;
- **the horizon record**: for each fitted component, its fitted support and the declared rule outside it.

**The shared holdout registry.** Track B's holdout registry is the single registry of held-out years for every lane. It is `docs/design/track_b_holdout_registry.json` (schema `track_b_holdout_registry.v1`), started by Track B's B0.2 audit (draft PR #554) on branch `trackb-b0-2-20261021` at `e1c3c1e6`, file SHA-256 `aa5ce9bbb127fdd1f0729bb8af2cdc66aefe74ec897010f9e80bababfeaa002c`, and moving to its own pull request on master. Every OP record cites it by path, commit and SHA-256 together, because the hash changes with each appended entry. Its order is fixed:
1. J's candidate years are recorded first.
2. OP0.1 and OP0.2 register each of their picks there before freezing them, and choose from the years that remain.
3. Any year an OP gate must share with J or another lane is disclosed by name in both registrations.

This matters most for `gate_ssa_totals` (OP12), whose SSA Annual Statistical Supplement years are the kind J needs for its independent holdout.

OP0.1 must also establish whether OACT publishes single-age fertility and single-age population projections; the public single-year tables do not (§2.7). Where it does not, the margin uses a registered bridge (the TR2026 baseline's derived shape, or CBO's single-age rates) or stays `not_available`.

### 5.3 Proposed numerical limits and tolerance classes

**Proposals only, subject to OP0's prospective feasibility and power review**, which may rescope before any candidate result and may never loosen a limit after one.

Each gated cell's tolerance comes from the class of its reference source, plus the uncertainty of the candidate that is compared with it. OP0 assigns every cell a reference class and a candidate procedure before its gate is approved; no class borrows another's procedure.

| Class | Sources | Reference-side uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Survey microdata | ACS and CPS ASEC files | The survey design: replicate weights where the file publishes them, otherwise household-disjoint half-splits of the file itself, under the repository's floor convention (statistic \|ln(candidate / reference)\|, tolerance round(mean + 4·sd, 3), at least 20 events on the weaker half, stabilized tolerance at most T_max = ln(1.5); `gates.yaml:1336`, `:1001–1006`) |
| Administrative and vital-statistics aggregates | NCHS natality counts and rates; SSA supplement tables | No sampling floor: a named allowance for measurement, concept, coverage and vintage, such as the dispersion of revisions to the same target year across published vintages plus half the published rounding unit, after the approach issue #151 proposed for gate w1's administrative anchors. Changes between outcome years, such as annual demographic or benefit growth, are not measurement uncertainty. Household half-splits are never manufactured from aggregate tables |
| Census counts | The 2020 Census | A named coverage allowance, from the Census Bureau's published coverage measurement for 2020 (pinned at OP0.1) |

**Candidate-side uncertainty is gate-specific.** Each gate specifies uncertainty for its actual start, donor and fitted-law inputs, respecting survey design and family clusters, plus simulation uncertainty. OP5 household resampling applies only to candidates projected from OP5's start (`gate_birth`, `gate_pop`); `gate_imm` resamples OP3's ACS donor files by their replicate design; `gate_entrant_earn` resamples OP3's entry bundles and carries Track B's law's estimation uncertainty; `gate_young_earn` resamples its own CPS and EPUF constructions; OP12 resamples the Microcosm frame by its own design. OP0 writes each gate's procedure into its frozen record.

For every class: simultaneous 95 percent bounds with Bonferroni over each registered cell family; Monte Carlo error below one-tenth of each tolerance; insufficient precision is inconclusive, never a pass (Track B §5.2). Cells that fail eligibility are report-only by a machine partition fixed before any candidate runs.

- **Alignment fidelity** (OP6, OP9): the aligned margin within its selection-resolution floor plus a numeric tolerance pinned in target units (#217 §6.1–6.2). Displacement corridors are authored before release; until a corridor artifact exists, each run publishes raw displacement with `corridor_status: not_available`. No target or corridor is fitted to OP12's held-out SSA outcomes.
- **Horizon drift** (OP10b): none proposed. OP0.2 freezes limits on drift growth for the backtest.

**The insured-status rule (Track B's rules lane, under d1802; a separate PR once #530 merges).** Its scope, as the coordinator relayed it on 21 October 2026: quarters of coverage; fully, currently and disability insured, including the special rules before 31 and for blindness; and the date last insured, designed for B6a's person-month risk set. Its interface, as relayed the same day: each year of a person's record carries a provenance, `OBSERVED`, `CONVENTION_FILLED` with a convention id, or `UNKNOWN`; the rule bounds `UNKNOWN` years at zero and at the maximum quarters of coverage; where both bounds give the same status it returns that status flagged "decided despite unknowns", with the unknown years listed; where they disagree it refuses by name (for example `unknown_earnings_years_decide_status`) and keeps the row in the denominator. The flag and refusal names are as the coordinator relayed them; OP8 checks this section against the rules lane's interface section when it lands. OP8(d) consumes the rule and does not change it. What OP8 supplies to it, and needs from it:

1. **Per-year provenance for quarters of coverage, from OP8(c).** Years whose covered earnings the entrant's history holds, reported in a source or projected after entry, go to the rule as `OBSERVED`; OP8 also records each year's origin (reported, or generated by the projection), so projected years stay labeled as model-generated even though the rule treats their values as known within the simulated scenario. Pre-entry years follow Max's decision 4 (§10): under the **zero convention** (a first-entry assumption) they go as `CONVENTION_FILLED` with zero quarters and a registered convention id; under the **censored convention** they go as `UNKNOWN`. OP8 supplies annual quarters, never monthly earnings. From 1978 on, quarters are credited from a year's covered earnings, up to four (`min_benefit_track_m/coverage.py:16–19`). The rule assigns them to calendar quarters under SSA's flexible assignment, which may place a quarter wherever it is most advantageous to the worker (POMS RS 00301.230), within the limits of RS 00301.240: a quarter counts once, none after the quarter of death, none inside a period of disability except its first and last quarters, and none in the future.
2. **The facts other than quarters that the statute needs, each with the same provenance.** Fully and currently insured status carry an additional criterion for anyone not a US citizen or national (Social Security Act §214(c)). For noncitizens and nonnationals, the rule first determines whether the additional requirements apply. Workers originally assigned an SSN before 1 January 2004 are exempt. Otherwise the rule applies POMS RS 00301.102: a Social Security number issued for work purposes at any time since then, or admission at any time as a business visitor or crewman, after which all covered earnings count (RS 00301.102 B.1–B.4, read 23 October 2026). Unknown applicability receives the same bounding or named-refusal treatment as other unavailable statutory facts. Elapsed years exclude years in a period of disability (§214(a)), and disability-insured status has a special rule for a prior period of disability that began before 31 and an exception for statutory blindness (§223(c)(1)). For entrants:
   - citizenship or national status comes from the donor's ACS citizenship item, `OBSERVED` as of the donor's interview and fixed thereafter (§3.6);
   - for noncitizens, the date their first Social Security number was assigned is `UNKNOWN` under the censored convention, because a reported year of entry cannot establish it; under the zero convention, which assumes first entry, an entrant arriving after 2003 can carry a `CONVENTION_FILLED` assignment date after 2003 under the same registered convention id. Where the requirements then apply, whether a work-purpose number was issued or a business-visitor or crewman admission occurred is `UNKNOWN`, because no public survey records it and this design models no legal status (#218 §1.3);
   - statutory blindness is `UNKNOWN`: survey vision items are not the statutory definition;
   - prior periods of disability are `UNKNOWN` for immigrants under both conventions unless independently supported, because neither convention speaks to disability history, and none for newborns.

   OP8 asks the rule to bound an `UNKNOWN` fact as it bounds an `UNKNOWN` year: return a status only where every admissible value gives the same one, and otherwise refuse by name. **The zero convention resolves uncertainty about pre-entry quarters of coverage only. Where another fact required by the applicable statutory test is unavailable, OP8 preserves the rule's named refusal and the entrant's denominator weight.**
3. **Elapsed years from the birth date, not the entry date.** The fully-insured test counts one quarter for each year elapsed after 1950 or, if later, after the year the person turned 21, and before the year of death or of turning 62, with at least 6 quarters, or 40 quarters in all (§214(a)), wherever the person lived. An immigrant who arrives at 45 therefore faces the same count as a native-born worker of the same age. OP8 supplies the birth date, and the rule evaluates at a stated month, as B6a needs. For disability-insured status the fully-insured count instead runs to the evaluation month, as if the person had turned 62 and applied on its first day (§223(c)(1)(A)).
4. **Monotone in quarters, given the other facts.** With the facts of point 2 and the disability-period history held fixed, adding a quarter of coverage in any year never takes away any insured status and never makes the date last insured earlier. The rule's bounding relies on this, so OP8 asks that the rule's tests state it as a property.
5. **Each output bounded separately.** Fully insured, currently insured, disability insured and the date last insured are each decided or refused on their own: two agreeing current statuses can coexist with different historical dates last insured, and the date is then refused even when the status is returned.
6. **An optional input for credits from outside US coverage,** empty by default, so that a later treatment of totalization agreements (§3.6) can add credits without changing the rule.
7. **Refusals stay refusals.** OP8(d) passes the rule's result through unchanged: a status, with its flag where unknowns were bounded, or the rule's named refusal. A refused entrant stays outside claiming and DI, as `entrant_domains.py` keeps entrants now, stays in every denominator and is counted by refusal reason; no refusal becomes "not insured".
8. **Newborns and emigrants.** A newborn has no pre-entry years; its quarters come from its own earnings under OP8(e), and a newborn citizen meets §214(c) by citizenship. An emigrant's quarters stop accruing at exit; the rule can still evaluate the emigrant's status and date last insured at later months, which OP12 needs to count insured emigrants.

## 7. Invariants and property-based tests per component

Each invariant becomes a Hypothesis property test on invented data beside the example tests, as the repository's rule for load-bearing logic requires. Two are differential tests between independent implementations.

- **Stock-flow (OP1).** For every year: people at the end = people at the start − deaths − emigrants + births + scheduled entrants, exactly in counts; and the same in weights, within the summation residual `reconcile_period` reports. Every addition carries a known `entry_kind`; every exit carries one exit kind; no person exits twice; no ID is reused (`validate_projection_allocator` on the exact metadata).
- **Emigration coupling (OP1, OP4).** With emigration off, the survivors equal the closed run's exactly. No person both dies and emigrates in one year. For a fixed post-mortality risk set, fixed exit units and coupled draws, increasing emigration probabilities cannot remove a same-year exit selected under the lower probabilities. This is a property of one year's transition only: across a projection, more exits in early years shrink later risk sets, so later-year emigrant weight can fall.
- **Horizon prefix (OP1).** Under the registered-horizon convention, the slice for year t is identical across runs that differ only in the horizon read; a production request with any other end year is refused. The committed evidence (§2.9) is the failing case this property guards.
- **Determinism and order invariance (all).** The same inputs and seeds give byte-identical output; shuffling roster rows changes nothing.
- **Newborn packet (OP2).** Each newborn has exactly one mother on the roster in its birth year; a linked second parent is on the roster, is not the mother and is her spouse or partner in that year's authoritative state; age 0 and birth year equal the year; weight equals the mother's; adult-domain markers are out of domain.
- **Live-roster fertility (OP2, differential).** Given identical support, initial parity and per-woman-year random draws, the live-roster kernel's births equal those of one invocation of `simulate_maternal_births`. Persistent parity: it starts at the registered seed, rises by exactly one with each birth, never falls, and never resets within a projection; no birth falls outside the kernel's fertile ages.
- **Entrant schedule and timing bridge (OP3).** Scaling every control by k scales every entrant weight by k; a zero control adds no rows and consumes no IDs (already true of `build_entrant_schedule`, `entrant_schedule.py:282–294`); the donor composition is reproduced exactly; no entrant's pre-entry covered earnings are zero unless the registered convention says so. **No duplicate survival filtering:** on invented data, for a component the bridge marks as year-end stayers, the expected year-end entrant weight after the seam's mortality and emigration equals that component's control; a schedule that feeds the raw stayer control into full-year mortality fails this test.
- **Relational consistency (OP4, OP6).** Every death of a linked spouse produces exactly one widowhood; no emigration produces one; an aligned death updates relations exactly as a drawn death does.
- **Alignment (OP6, OP9).** The unaligned artifact is byte-identical whether the aligned producer is importable or not; the aligned margin is within its selection-resolution floor plus tolerance; the margin, year and stratum representation is complete, with known zeros and unavailable cells distinct, and its keys are identical on both sides; no person weight changes; an empty target bundle reproduces the unaligned run.
- **Stable population (OP10a, differential).** With constant single-age fertility and mortality and no migration, the expected age-sex distribution in each year (the mean over seeds) agrees, within Monte Carlo error, with a two-sex Leslie projection from the same start and rates under the engine's timing conventions (§2.2); and the long-run growth rate approaches the logarithm of the Leslie matrix's dominant eigenvalue. The micro engine and the matrix are the two implementations compared.
- **Extrapolation (OP10a, OP10b).** No component is queried outside its fitted support without a declared rule; the refusal is tested.
- **Insured-status provenance (OP8(c), (d)).** Every year and every non-quarter fact of an entrant's record carries exactly one provenance; under the zero convention no pre-entry year is `UNKNOWN`. Unknown years and facts remain disclosed. Each output is returned only when all admissible completions give the same result; otherwise the rule returns its named refusal. OP8 never substitutes an invented value for an unavailable input; OP8(d) never turns a refusal into a status; a refused entrant stays in every denominator; each of the four outputs is decided or refused on its own.
- **Entrant economics (OP8).** Earnings are finite and nonnegative; pre-entry years are never filled with zero unless the registered convention says so; the hand-off at 25 lands inside the forward law's support (a support check, not an admission of the transition); no unavailable year or fact is replaced by an invented value.

Counterexamples are executed and minimized before anyone calls them bugs.

## 8. Risks and kill criteria

### Blocking rule

Track B's rule applies unchanged (Track B §7): required-source unavailability, unresolved exposure, inadequate precision, unsupported critical configurations, or a failed gate or required regression test block the corresponding admission and the milestones that depend on it. Fallbacks register before candidate outcomes. Failed or unsupported cells stay reported, and removing them cannot manufacture a pass.

### Named conditions and what each stops

| Condition | Trigger | Stops |
|---|---|---|
| `ACCOUNTING_IDENTITY_FAIL` | A stock-flow identity fails in any year | Every OP run until fixed |
| `ID_OR_STREAM_COLLISION` | An allocator could reuse an ID, or a production run's end year differs from its registered horizon | That run |
| `CONTROL_SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE` | A target or control (OACT single-age fertility or population, V.A2 rows) cannot be pinned | That margin stays `not_available`; no "aligned to OACT" claim for it |
| `UNIVERSE_BRIDGE_UNRESOLVED` | No registered bridge from the Social Security area to the roster | Real-data immigrant schedules and every stock claim |
| `TIMING_BRIDGE_UNRESOLVED` | No registered component-specific timing and exposure bridge | Every production immigrant schedule |
| `DONOR_SUPPORT_THIN` | `gate_imm`'s cells fail support floors after the registered pooling ladder | Gated composition claims for those cells; they stay report-only |
| `EMIGRATION_UNIDENTIFIED` | OP0.1 finds no admissible holdout for exits | Composition claims by nativity and duration; totals stay aligned |
| `HOLDOUT_EXPOSED` | OP0 cannot show a gate's observations or estimands unused (as for NCHS's 2016–2024 rates and the EPUF arm) | Independent-prediction admission for that gate; it reports as regression evidence |
| `ALIGNMENT_DISPLACEMENT_EXCESSIVE` | Displacement above its corridor in a registered number of consecutive years, or growing through the horizon | Release of that aligned margin pending model review; a second registered review that finds the same blocks any claim past 2030 that depends on the margin until its law is refit |

### Attempt ledger

Track B's cumulative attempt ledger applies by mechanism and gate family: open-population fertility, immigrant composition, emigration, demographic composition, entrant earnings and young-adult earnings each count separately. Renaming a module does not reset a counter; two failed successors pause a mechanism pending new evidence.

### Risks most likely to slip

- **Track B's B2 successor.** Every economic milestone waits on it (§3.4), and two failures pause the earnings mechanism.
- **OP12's external prerequisites.** Gate 3 waits on parked d947; G part 2, I and the insured-status rule are not yet built; and spouse and survivor types wait on the Microcosm start's T2 marital-makeup condition.
- **Holdout competition with J.** `gate_ssa_totals` and Track B's J may want the same SSA supplement years. The shared registry (§5.2) gives J first choice; if too few unexposed years remain, OP12 registers on shared years with disclosure, or as regression evidence.

- **Insured status for noncitizen entrants.** Without a bridge for §214(c)'s facts (§3.6), the rule may refuse many noncitizen entrants' statuses, so their benefit outputs may stay largely missing.
- **Granular demographic targets.** If OACT's single-age fertility and population are neither public nor obtainable, alignment uses registered bridges or stays partial.
- **The universe and timing bridges and V.A2's semantics.** The temporary-or-unlawfully-present inflow counts people who remain to year-end (`entrant_schedule.py:365–371`), and adjustments of status move people between categories without moving them in or out.
- **ACS stock proxies.** Recent arrivals in the ACS are survivors and stayers observed after entry, not arrivals (#218 §2.2).
- **Weight concentration and run size.** Seventy-five years of an open population on a Microcosm-sized frame is a larger run than any so far; OP1 measures memory and runtime on invented data at full size before OP10a.
